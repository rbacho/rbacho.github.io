Cryptography & distributed computing

Renas Bacho

I am a postdoctoral researcher in the group of Eike Kiltz at Ruhr University Bochum, where I work primarily on threshold signatures, distributed key generation, and Byzantine consensus. I also study related topics such as multi-signatures, blind signatures, and verifiable secret sharing.

I received my PhD in computer science from Saarland University in 2026. I was based at the CISPA Helmholtz Center for Information Security and advised by Julian Loss. My PhD thesis was titled Enhancing the Security and Efficiency of Distributed Key Generation and Its Applications. Before that, I studied mathematics at Humboldt University of Berlin. My master’s thesis was on modular representations of Borel subgroups over non-Archimedean local fields. My bachelor’s thesis was on the maximal genus of smooth algebraic curves.

Research papers

Research

Current questions

  • How efficient can threshold signatures be while achieving strong adaptive security from standard assumptions?
  • How much communication and how many rounds does distributed key generation require in an asynchronous network?
  • How quickly can Byzantine agreement and broadcast terminate in synchronous networks when few parties are faulty?

Research papers

For more work, see Google Scholar.

  • Preprint · 2026

    Early-Stopping Dishonest Majority Broadcast with Optimal Round Complexity

    with Kecheng Shi

    We give a deterministic broadcast protocol that stops in at most min{f + 3, t + 1} rounds even with a dishonest majority, matching the lower bound where it applies. Here, f is the actual number of faults and t is the fault bound.

  • Preprint · 2026

    Strongly Adaptive Asynchronous Byzantine Agreement with Linear Communication and Almost-Everywhere Termination

    with Simon Holmgaard Kamp, Julian Loss, Jesper Buus Nielsen, and Maciej Obremski

    We give an asynchronous agreement protocol with linear expected communication that terminates for almost all honest parties in constant expected rounds, even when the adversary can retract undelivered messages upon corruption.

  • Preprint · 2026

    Concrete LDVR Attacks with Fewer Signers

    I show that concrete attacks on the low-dimensional vector representation problem, which underlies the adaptive security of some threshold Schnorr schemes, apply with substantially fewer signers than previously demonstrated.

  • ASIACRYPT 2026

    Tightly and Adaptively Secure Two-Round Threshold Signatures from DDH

    with Yanbo Chen

    We construct TZAR, a pairing-free two-round threshold signature scheme with a tight proof of adaptive security from decisional Diffie–Hellman.

  • ACM CCS 2026

    Schnorr Signatures and MuSig2 Are Jointly Secure, Even in Deterministic Wallets

    with Yanbo Chen, Poulami Das, Julian Loss, Tim Ruffing, and Benedikt Wagner

    We prove joint security for Schnorr signatures and MuSig2 when they share a signing key, including under the key derivation used in deterministic wallets.

  • DISC 2026

    Quadratic Asynchronous DKG from Plain Setup

    with Ittai Abraham and Gilad Stern

    We achieve asynchronous distributed key generation with quadratic communication and constant round complexity from a plain public-key setup, even against strongly adaptive corruptions.

    Invited to the special issue of Distributed Computing.

  • CRYPTO 2026

    Adaptively Secure (Aggregatable) PVSS from Standard Assumptions

    with Yanbo Chen and Julian Loss

    We build adaptively secure, publicly verifiable secret sharing from standard assumptions in a plain public-key model. One variant lets anyone combine multiple sharing transcripts into a single verifiable transcript.

  • ACM PODC 2026

    Nearly Quadratic Asynchronous Distributed Key Generation from Recursive Consensus

    with Ittai Abraham, Julian Loss, and Gilad Stern

    We use recursive consensus to achieve asynchronous distributed key generation with nearly quadratic expected communication and a tunable trade-off between communication and rounds.

    Invited to the special issue of Distributed Computing.

  • EUROCRYPT 2026

    Adaptively Secure Partially Non-Interactive Threshold Schnorr Signatures in the AGM

    with Yanbo Chen, Julian Loss, Stefano Tessaro, and Chenzhi Zhu

    We introduce ms-FROST, a two-round threshold Schnorr scheme that supports offline preprocessing, and prove full adaptive security in the algebraic group model.

  • EUROCRYPT 2026

    Earpicks: Tightly Secure Two-Round Multi- and Threshold Signatures

    with Yanbo Chen

    We give tightly secure two-round multi-signatures and threshold signatures without pairings. The multi-signature supports key aggregation and uses only three field elements and one bit.

  • Nature 2026

    A benchmark of expert-level academic questions to assess AI capabilities

    with the Center for AI Safety, Scale AI, and the HLE Contributors Consortium

    We introduce Humanity’s Last Exam, a benchmark of 2,500 expert-written questions across dozens of fields for evaluating language models.

Personal

In high school, I enjoyed taking part in mathematics competitions. At graduation, I was admitted to the German Mathematical Society (DMV) and the German Physical Society (DPG) in recognition of my achievements in mathematics and physics, respectively.

  • Bundeswettbewerb Mathematik

    Bundeswettbewerb Mathematik is one of Germany’s most prestigious school mathematics competitions. I won first and second prizes in the second round in different years and received a special award in the 2013 national final.

  • Digitaler Mathekalender

    The calendar features challenging mathematical problems inspired by real-world applications, from optimizing train timetables to planning collision-free delivery routes. I solved all 24 problems in multiple editions and placed in the top 0.5% each time.

  • German Mathematical Olympiad

    The Olympiad is one of Germany’s most prestigious school mathematics competitions. I won second and third prizes in the North Rhine-Westphalia state round and took part in Germany’s selection exams for the International Mathematical Olympiad in multiple years.

  • IMC Prosperity Global Trading Challenge

    Participants write trading algorithms and make manual trading decisions in a simulated market. My team finished in the top 2% of about 12,000 teams in the 15-day challenge.
